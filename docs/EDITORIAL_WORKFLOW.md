# MARCOS evidence-led editorial workflow

This is the operating standard for MARCOS Insights. It turns the editorial brief into a repeatable human-reviewed process. Automation may assist research, comparison, drafting, and QA; it may not invent evidence, verify a person by inference, or publish without a human attestation.

## 1. Brief and search intent

Before drafting, record:

- the exact topic, audience, country, and reader decision the article supports;
- one primary query, four to eight secondary queries, and natural-language questions;
- the intended search intent: informational, comparative, or decision-support;
- the jurisdictions and dates that make a claim perishable;
- the primary entity, related entities, and the article's one-sentence information gain.

Keyword volume, competition, CPC, and SERP features must be marked as directional unless they came from a named, dated data source. Never fabricate precision.

## 2. Author and reviewer assignment

The admin editor uses a stable MARCOS author profile and records a public evidence URL for that person's relevant role or expertise. Each article also needs a named human reviewer, a concise explanation of why that reviewer fits the topic, and a public evidence URL.

An internal title is not proof of market performance, a regulated status, an academic credential, or years of experience. Do not state any of those without direct evidence.

For financial, legal, tax, or regulatory material, select a reviewer whose evidenced expertise matches the claim. If that evidence is unavailable, the article stays in `review`.

## 3. Source pack and competitor benchmark

Build the evidence pack before the draft:

1. Prefer regulators, exchanges, government portals, statutes, issuer filings, audited reports, official product documentation, and original datasets.
2. Record at least three primary or authoritative URLs in the article editor.
3. Benchmark at least five relevant results or direct competitors. Note their structure, evidence quality, unanswered questions, stale claims, and trust gaps.
4. Turn the gap analysis into an original outline. Do not copy headings mechanically.
5. Re-check every current number, rule, fee, date, threshold, and product term immediately before review.

Secondary sources may help discovery, but material claims should resolve to the strongest available primary source.

## 4. Draft standard

The article must:

- answer the reader's core question early;
- distinguish fact, interpretation, example, and opinion;
- explain material terms at first use;
- use descriptive headings and concise, answerable sections;
- state uncertainty instead of filling gaps with plausible text;
- include visible methodology, limitations, conflicts, and source links;
- avoid guaranteed returns, urgency tactics, fabricated testimonials, disguised advertising, and personalised advice.

Unresolved notes such as `[VERIFY]`, `[AUTHOR PROOF REQUIRED]`, `[SOURCE REQUIRED]`, and `[INSERT TOPIC]` are allowed in drafts and review copies. The application blocks them from publication.

## 5. Human QA and status transitions

### Draft

Incomplete research and editorial markers are allowed. A draft is never public.

### Review

The assigned editor checks:

- every material claim against the source pack;
- author and reviewer evidence;
- query intent, title, description, canonical URL, and image metadata;
- visible risks, limitations, conflicts, and dated claims;
- Article/Person structured data against the visible page;
- mobile readability, links, images, and accessibility;
- plagiarism, unsupported certainty, and accidental advice language.

### Published

Publication is rejected unless all evidence fields are complete, at least three source URLs are present, no unresolved markers remain, and an admin checks the human-attestation box for that exact version. Material edits to a published article require fresh attestation and update the review date.

### Archived

Archive content that can no longer be supported or safely updated. Do not leave stale high-stakes content live merely to preserve traffic.

## 6. Quality scorecard

Score each item from 0 to 2. A published article should score at least 18/20 and must not score 0 on evidence, safety, author fit, or review.

| Dimension                 | 0             | 1                | 2                                               |
| ------------------------- | ------------- | ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Intent match              | unclear       | partial          | directly resolves the reader decision           |
| Primary evidence          | absent        | mixed            | material claims trace to authoritative sources  |
| Original value            | derivative    | modest synthesis | clear information gain                          |
| Author fit                | unsupported   | role only        | article-specific public evidence                |
| Reviewer fit              | absent        | named only       | qualification and evidence match the topic      |
| Accuracy/currentness      | stale/unknown | partly dated     | current claims re-checked and dated             |
| Safety/compliance         | misleading    | generic caveat   | risks and boundaries are specific and prominent |
| Limitations/conflicts     | absent        | generic          | material limitations and interests are explicit |
| Page/structured data      | inconsistent  | partial          | visible content and schema agree                |
| Readability/accessibility | poor          | usable           | scannable, mobile-safe, and accessible          |
